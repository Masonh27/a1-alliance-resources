Slide 01 — Why AI Automation

The demand is here. The timing is now.

TIME

The average small business owner spends 20+ hours a week on tasks that could run automatically. Follow-ups, scheduling, quoting, reminders — AI handles all of it. You show up for the work only you can do.

MONEY

It protects and generates revenue.

Missed calls, slow follow-up, zero re-engagement — these are silent revenue leaks. AI automation plugs them. Businesses that implement now are capturing jobs their competitors are losing every single day.

GROWTH

It's only getting bigger.

AI automation isn't a trend — it's the operating system of every competitive business in the next 5 years. The market is accelerating. Businesses that move now build the advantage that late movers will spend years trying to close.

The Bottom Line

AI automation gives business owners time, money, and a competitive edge — and it's only growing.

Slide 03 — The Real Product

AI doesn't solve the problem. We do.

This is the most important thing to understand. AI is a tool. We are the people who know how to use it. What we're really selling is our ability to find the problem and build the right solution.

We find the bottleneck first

Before we touch a single tool, we identify exactly what's costing the business the most — in time, in money, in missed opportunity. That's the problem we solve.

We sell us — not the technology

Clients don't hire us because they understand AI. They hire us because they trust us to understand their business and fix what's broken. The relationship is the product.

AI is the vehicle, not the destination

We use VAPI, n8n, Claude, GoHighLevel — but none of that matters to the client. What matters is that their phone gets answered, their leads get followed up, and their calendar fills itself.

Results over technology, always

Never lead with the tool. Lead with the outcome. "You'll never miss an after-hours call again" beats "we'll set up a VAPI voice agent" every single time.

Wrong way to pitch

"We build AI voice agents using VAPI integrated with n8n workflows and CRM automation pipelines..."

Right way to pitch

"You're losing jobs every week to missed calls. We fix that — and your phone answers itself at 9 PM on a Sunday."

Slide 04 — The Framework

How to find the problem and build the solution.

This is the three-step process we use on every client. Define the problem. Evaluate what fixing it is worth. Then build.

Step 01
🔍

Define

Find the bottleneck that's costing the most. Ask the right questions — where are you losing time? Where are leads falling through? What's the one thing you wish ran itself? That answer is your build target.

Step 02
⚖️

Evaluate

Tie the problem to real money. How many calls are you missing a week? What's the average job value? If you fix this — what does it return? The ROI has to be clear before you pitch anything.

Step 03
⚙️

Build

Design the simplest system that solves the problem. Don't overbuild. A 3-step workflow that works beats a 9-step system every time. Simplify first. Automate last. Ship fast.

Slide 05 — The Money

Three ways to make money in AI automation.

You don't have to be a developer to win in this space. Here are the three main roles — and how each one generates real income.

Path 01 — Sales

The Closer

You don't build anything. You find the businesses that need this, run the discovery, close the deal, and hand it to the dev team. Commission-based. High upside. Pure sales.

  • Outreach, cold calls, discovery calls
  • Qualify the problem into the right offer
  • Close and hand off — then go again
  • No technical knowledge required
  • Income scales directly with your reps
Path 02 — Development

The Builder

You build the systems. Voice agents, nurture workflows, CRM automations, custom AI logic. Every build a sales rep closes lands on your desk. Project fees plus ongoing maintenance revenue.

  • Build the systems clients pay for
  • Tools: VAPI, n8n, Claude API, GHL
  • Project-based + monthly retainer income
  • Builds compound — systems need maintenance
  • The higher the complexity, the higher the fee
Path 03 — Founder

The Operator

You run the agency. You hire the sales reps. You hire the devs. You manage the client relationships and build the machine. Every deal your team closes flows through you.

  • Build the team around you
  • Own the client relationships and delivery
  • Layer in recurring revenue as the base
  • Scale through people, not just skills
  • This is the long game — highest ceiling

Q&A Highlights — What Got Asked, What Got Answered

Where do you find people to hire?

Facebook groups, Upwork (especially for developers), Fiverr, personal network, and your own community. Mason's first developer is in college. His sales rep is a personal friend. Lead with who you know and who's hungry. Don't hire until you know the process yourself — you need to be able to show them what right looks like.

Are you selling automations or yourself as the operator? (Basil)

You're selling yourself, your outcomes, and your trust. Not the automation. Business owners don't buy things they don't understand — but they will buy from someone standing in front of them making a real promise. The automation is the delivery vehicle. You are the product.

How do you price? Per workflow, retainer, or outcome-based? (Basil)

Base it on: (1) how long it takes to build, (2) what it costs to run, (3) developer maintenance time, (4) what the market charges, (5) making sure everyone on your team makes money. Mason splits it: sales gets a %, developer gets a %, founder reinvests the rest. Start cheap, prove it, then raise by 30%.

If starting from zero, what do you study first? (Zain)

Learn n8n (the automation layer) and Claude Code (how you actually build and integrate AI). Watch Nick Sarav's YouTube — he has a 6-hour n8n course and a 4-hour Claude Code course. Both are linked in the Discord. APIs and JSON will make sense as you go — you don't need to study them separately first.

What's a good first project to build? (Zain)

An email summarizer. Build a workflow in n8n that reads your email, uses AI to summarize it, and sends that summary to a Discord channel. That's Mason's "AI Insights" channel that fires every morning. It reads, summarizes, and sends — completely automated. Simple, real, and proves the concept.

What industries and niches work best?

Service-based businesses that aren't going anywhere — HVAC, roofing, plumbing, Medicare, auto detailing, home services. Look for businesses that run on leads, speed, and repeat customers. They spend money on marketing to get leads and then lose those leads to slow response times. That's your entry point.

How do you handle client dashboards? (Basil)

Mason builds live HTML dashboards hosted on Railway. Shows the client what's being built, current progress (0/5 systems complete, etc.), notes, and time frames. All built in Claude or Claude Code, pushed to Railway, client gets a link. Real-time updates — Mason just edits it as he goes and it reflects live on the client's end.

What does AI voice agent actually sound like to the customer? (Billy)

Like a human. It has a custom prompt, set tone, and full conversation logic. It can answer FAQs, give quotes, book directly to the calendar, and if it doesn't know something — it transfers the call or says "I'll have someone call you back." 24/7. Never sick. No unanswered calls.

Is doing marketing + automation a good package to sell?

Yes — but get really good at one before you add the other. Marketing gets them in the door, automation keeps them. The risk is being mid at both. If you're going to package it, learn both properly first. Offer the second service for free to existing clients to test it, then upsell as you go.

How do you get hired with zero sales experience? (Tecee)

Mason doesn't need you to have experience — he has a training system. You go through the modules (offer stack, who we sell to, why we do it), pass a mock call with Mason, then get access to the CRM with real leads. He prefers teaching people fresh so the knowledge is clean and specific to AI automation.
